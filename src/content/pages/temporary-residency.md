## Quick summary

**Temporary residency** is a **legal status** that allows eligible non-citizens to **stay in Armenia beyond short visa-free or tourist visits** when they meet the rules for a recognized ground (employment, study, family ties, business, and other categories defined by law). People who plan to **live or work in Armenia medium- or long-term** often pursue it after completing earlier steps like **address registration** and obtaining a **social card**. **Processing times vary widely**—**confirm timelines and eligibility with the Migration Service or a qualified advisor**.

## Who needs this

- Non-citizens planning to **stay beyond their visa-free or tourist limit** (e.g. the 90-in-180 rolling window for many nationalities).
- People who want to **work legally, access long-term services**, or establish a more stable legal basis in Armenia.
- Not needed for short visits within visa-free allowances — check your limits with the [stay calculator](/documents/stay-calculator).

## Prerequisites

- **Address registration** — usually required before applying.
- **Social card** — typically required or strongly recommended.
- A **valid ground for application** (employment, study, family, business, etc.) — grounds and requirements differ.

## What you need

- **Passport** (original, valid for the duration of requested stay, plus copies).
- **Address registration confirmation**.
- **Social card** (in most cases).
- **Documents proving your ground** for residency (employment contract, university enrollment, family connection, etc.).
- **Application form** and **fees** — **[confirm current amounts and forms at the migration service or official channel]**.
- **Photos** — **[confirm format and quantity]**.

## Steps

1. **Complete prerequisites** — address registration and social card.
2. **Determine your ground** — employment, study, family, or other eligible category.
3. **Gather all required documents** — including proof of ground, passport copies, photos, and fee payment.
4. **Visit the Migration Service** (Passport and Visa Department of the Police, or the designated office).
5. **Submit your application** — staff will review; they may request additional documents.
6. **Wait for processing** — **[processing times vary; can range from weeks to months — confirm locally]**.
7. **Receive your decision** — if approved, you'll receive a temporary residency card or permit.

## Warnings

- **Start early** — do not wait until your visa-free days are nearly exhausted. Processing takes time.
- Requirements **change** — always verify current rules with the Migration Service or a trusted legal resource.
- **Grounds matter** — not every reason qualifies. If unsure, seek guidance before applying.
- An **expired or denied** application can complicate your status — understand the process before starting.
- This guide provides general orientation; for **legal advice specific to your case**, consult a qualified professional.
