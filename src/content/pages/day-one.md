## Quick summary

Your first day is about **safety, connectivity, and cash**—not finishing every bureaucratic step. Get a **SIM**, secure **AMD**, reach your **accommodation**, and install **essential apps**; leave heavy paperwork for the days after you have slept and oriented yourself.

## What to do first

1. **Get a SIM card** — At the airport or in the city; bring your passport; major operators include **Ucom**, **VivaCell-MTS**, and **Team** (names and plans change—confirm at the counter).
2. **Exchange money or use an ATM** — Have **some AMD cash** for taxis and small purchases; rates vary—compare if you have time.
3. **Get to your accommodation** — Follow [Airport to city](/newcomer/airport-to-city) if you land at Zvartnots; have your address on a map or in Armenian if possible.
4. **Download essential apps** — Ride-share, maps, and messaging—see [Essential apps](/daily-life/essential-apps).
5. **Rest and orient** — You do not need to solve residency or housing contracts on hour one.

## SIM card options

Armenia has several mobile operators; **Ucom**, **VivaCell-MTS**, and **Team** are commonly used. You will **typically** need your **passport** for registration. Airport kiosks are convenient but may have fewer plan choices than city shops—**compare data packages** if you care about price.

## Money and cash

The currency is the **Armenian dram (AMD)**. **Exchange offices** and **ATMs** are widely available in Yerevan; **cash** is still used everywhere, so keep small notes for drivers and corner shops. Card acceptance varies—do not assume every taxi or market takes cards.

## What can wait

- **Address registration** — Important, but rarely urgent on day one—see [Address registration](/documents/address-registration) when you have a stable address.
- **Social card and bank account** — Usually follow after address registration—[Documents](/documents) hub for order of operations.
- **Full job search or long housing viewings** — After you are rested and connected.
