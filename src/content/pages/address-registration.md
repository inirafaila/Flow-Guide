## Quick summary

Address registration (often referred to locally as **propiska**) is the process of recording where you live with the authorities. For most non-citizens staying in Armenia beyond a short visit, it is a routine step toward legal residence paperwork. Completing it early usually makes later steps—especially the **social card**—smoother. Processing time is **typically a few business days** once documents are accepted, but may vary by office load.

## Who needs this

- Most **non-citizens** planning to stay beyond a short tourist-style visit should register their address if they have a lawful place to stay.
- **Address registration is typically required before** you can apply for a **social card** (and is referenced in many other residence-related processes).

## What you need

- **Passport** (original and a copy).
- Proof of address—commonly a **rental agreement** or **landlord notarized consent** (requirements vary).
- A **completed application form** (forms are usually available at the office; versions may change).
- **[Fee, if applicable]** — confirm current fee at the office or official channel before you go.

## Steps

1. **Gather documents** — Check the latest list for your situation (nationality and housing type); bring originals and copies as required.
2. **Visit the local police passport department** (or the responsible community office in some areas) that serves your registered address.
3. **Submit your application** — Staff will review your file; they may ask for corrections or additional papers.
4. **Receive your registration** — You typically get a stamp or certificate in your passport or a separate slip; **processing time is often a few business days** but can be longer at busy times.

## Warnings

- Do **not** delay registration unnecessarily — staying without registering when you are required to can **complicate future applications** and residency steps.
- Requirements **may differ by region and office**; what worked for someone else may not match your case.
- If your **landlord is uncooperative**, seek guidance from official sources or community groups you trust—some situations need a different housing proof path.
