## Quick summary

You can find housing **through a landlord directly** (listings, referrals, community groups) or **via an agency** that shows properties and sometimes helps with paperwork. Neither path guarantees safety or fairness—**both** can be professional or sloppy. This page compares **what typically differs** so you can ask better questions. **It is not legal advice**; for contracts and disputes, use **official sources** or a **qualified adviser**.

## Direct vs agency — what changes

**Direct (landlord)**

- **Pros (typical):** Potentially **lower total cost** if there is **no agency commission**; sometimes **faster** back-and-forth on small repairs when you have the owner’s phone.
- **Cons (typical):** **Variable** contract quality; you do more of your own **due diligence** on identity of the person signing and on the property’s real state.

**Agency**

- **Pros (typical):** **More viewings** in one day; someone may **mediate** language gaps; some agents keep **standard templates** (still read them).
- **Cons (typical):** **Fees**—often a **percentage of one month’s rent** or a fixed service fee—**confirm in writing** who pays, when, and for what. **Quality varies widely** between offices.

**Neither** replaces your own checks: **see the unit**, **read the draft contract**, and **do not pay** large deposits to strangers without traceable steps you are comfortable with.

## Viewings and paperwork

- **View in person** when possible—photos and pins can omit noise, heating issues, or building access.
- **Ask who owns** the flat and **who will sign**; mismatches between ad, keys, and signer are a reason to **pause** and clarify.
- **ID and proof** habits differ; some landlords want copies of passport pages—**know what you are sharing** and with whom.
- **Address registration** is a separate official process from a private lease—plan timing with [Address registration](/documents/address-registration) guidance; **requirements vary** by situation.

## Deposits and contracts (high level)

- **Deposit (caution money)** — amounts and **refund rules** differ; **get refund conditions in writing** (normal wear vs damage, notice period).
- **Rent amount, due date, currency** — confirm **AMD vs other** if someone quotes both; note **how** utilities are billed.
- **Duration and notice** — fixed term vs monthly; **penalties** for early exit—**read before signing**.
- **Inventory** — photos/video dated at **move-in** help later disagreements; **this is practical advice, not a legal review**.

**Confirm locally** anything that sounds unusual for the market you are in.

## Scams and pressure tactics (general warnings)

- **Urgent “wire now or lose it”** without a viewing or signed draft you have read.
- **Payments to random personal accounts** with no written link to the property or landlord name on the contract.
- **Duplicate listings** at very different prices across sites—**reverse-image search** and compare.
- **Sole reliance on verbal promises** about repairs, furniture, or registration help—**put material terms in the contract** or an addendum you both sign.

If something feels off, **slow down**—community forums are full of **anecdotes**, not guarantees.
