## Quick summary

Many newcomers earn money through **delivery, ride-share, task apps, or freelance** while longer-term plans take shape. Income can start quickly, but **rates, fees, and paperwork expectations vary** by platform, client, and your residency status. This page orients you to common paths and what to verify—**not** a promise of earnings or legal advice.

## Who this is for

- People who need **cash flow soon** after arrival and are open to **flexible or informal** arrangements.
- Anyone weighing **app-based work** against **formal employment** and trying to understand what is typically involved.

## What counts as “quick”

“Quick” here means **you can often start within days or weeks**, not that pay is high or stable. It usually involves **signing up in an app**, passing basic checks, and accepting jobs as they appear. **Formal payroll roles** often take longer because of contracts, registrations, and employer processes.

## Common paths

- **Delivery and courier work** — food and packages via platform apps; pay is often per trip or per hour bands defined in the app.
- **Ride-share** — driver partner programs where available; vehicle, insurance, and local rules apply.
- **Freelance and remote contracts** — design, tech, language, admin; paid by invoice, platform, or informal agreement.
- **Short-term or informal gigs** — sometimes arranged directly; **get terms in writing** when you can, even if the relationship is informal.

Names of apps and employers change; treat any example as **illustrative**, not an endorsement.

## What you typically need

Requirements depend on the platform or client, but plan for:

- **Valid ID** and, for many formal steps, progress on **residency-related paperwork**.
- **Address registration** before or alongside other document steps where offices require it — [Address registration](/documents/address-registration).
- **Social card** (public services number) for banking, many contracts, and tax-related processes — [Social card](/documents/social-card).
- A **local phone number** and often a **bank account** or accepted payout method; some apps pay to cards or wallets—confirm in the app.

If something is blocked because paperwork is incomplete, **Documents** is usually the path forward—not a shortcut around rules.

## Payments and cash

- **How you get paid** (card, transfer, cash, platform balance) affects fees and risk—see [Payments](/payments) for terminals, apps, and everyday money movement.
- **Cash-in-hand** still exists; it can be simpler short term but harder to trace for taxes or disputes. **This is not legal or tax advice**—when in doubt, confirm with a qualified professional or official guidance.

## Risks and realities

- **Contracts and terms** — read payout rules, deactivation policies, and fees; they change.
- **Cash vs. formal work** — informal pay may not come with benefits or protections you would get under a standard employment contract.
- **Expectations** — seasonal demand, language barriers, and vehicle costs can matter as much as “hours online.”
- **No legal advice** — employment, migration, and tax rules are fact-specific. Verify your situation with **official sources** or a qualified adviser.
